F1 2026
The Reset

New cars, new engines, an 11th team, and the biggest rulebook overhaul in the sport's history. 11 of 23 rounds in, here's what actually changed — and who's still stuck on zero points.

01Standings, after 11 of 23 rounds

Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes) leads with 6 wins, 6 poles, and 9 podiums from 11 races. Mercedes and Ferrari have been the season's biggest beneficiaries of the reset so far.

Source: F1-Fansite.com and F1 Daily Brief, standings after the Hungarian Grand Prix (round 11).

The constructors' table got reshuffled hard

2025 champions McLaren 1st → 3rd
Mercedes climbed to 1st (379 pts)
Red Bull dropped to 6th

Early-season snapshot after the regulation reset — the team that won everything in 2025 is now fighting to stay in the top 3, and two historically midfield teams are punching into the top 5.

The season itself changed shape too

  • 23 rounds instead of the planned 24 — Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were cancelled amid the regional conflict
  • A new Madrid Grand Prix debuts on the Madring street circuit
  • The Dutch Grand Prix (Zandvoort) runs for the last time this year — its contract wasn't renewed
  • Season runs March 8 (Australia) to December 6 (Abu Dhabi)

Same three drivers, full season profile

Points, wins, and podiums on the same 0–100 scale (points ÷2.2, wins ×10, podiums ×10, so all three sit on a comparable range). Antonelli's shape is round — strong everywhere. Hamilton's points lag despite a genuinely competitive podium rate once he found late-season form.

Source: cross-checked race-by-race (F1-Fansite, F1 Daily Brief, individual GP result pages) rather than a single aggregator, since standings trackers disagreed with each other more than once while building this.

Season points 0

The first fully American-owned works team in the modern F1 era, fielding two veteran drivers who'd both lost their seats the year before: Sergio Pérez (ex-Red Bull) and Valtteri Bottas (ex-Sauber).

The point that got away, in real time

At the Monaco Grand Prix, Pérez crossed the line 10th — Cadillac's first-ever points finish. Then race control reviewed the restart: he'd lined up out of position after the red flag. Penalty applied, dropped to P15. The point went to Fernando Alonso and Aston Martin instead. It remains the closest Cadillac has come to scoring all season.

Source: Formula1.com, "Half-term report: Cadillac's best and worst moments from 2026."

03What actually changed for 2026

  • Active aerodynamics replaces the old DRS flap — front and rear wings physically change shape on straights (low drag) and corners (high downforce)
  • New power units: roughly 50/50 split between electric and combustion power, up from a heavily combustion-dominated split before
  • MGU-H removed — an expensive component that added cost without much performance gain
  • 100% Advanced Sustainable Fuels across the whole grid
  • Cars are smaller, narrower, and lighter, aimed at closer racing on tight circuits like Monaco and Singapore

Source: Formula1.com official 2026 regulations guide; GPFans regulations explainer.

Corners vs. top speed: not the relationship you'd expect

Monza has the fewest corners on this list (11) and the highest top speed (362 km/h) — the "Temple of Speed" reputation, confirmed. Monaco sits at the opposite corner: 19 turns and barely 290 km/h anywhere on track. Baku is the outlier that breaks the pattern — 20 corners, a street circuit, and still north of 350 km/h thanks to one enormous straight.

Source: Motor Sport Magazine's per-circuit top-speed survey and FLOW Racers' corner-count breakdown, cross-checked against each other.

Fastest pit stop, race by race — and who beat the big teams

Austria → Racing Bulls (2.03s) · Barcelona → McLaren (2.13s) · Monaco → Mercedes (2.17s) · Britain → Mercedes (2.18s). Racing Bulls — a midfield team, not one of the giants — set the season's fastest recorded stop so far, quicker than either Mercedes or McLaren's own best efforts elsewhere in the year.

Source: Formula1.com DHL Fastest Pit Stop Award, individual race reports.

The cars got slower — by design, and it's verifiable lap by lap

Melbourne — 3.42s slower Spa — 3.80s slower
2026 pole time vs. 2025 pole time, same circuit

Same circuits, same qualifying format, one year apart. Russell's 2026 Australian GP pole was 3.4 seconds slower than Norris's 2025 benchmark; Antonelli's 2026 Belgian GP pole was 3.8 seconds slower than Norris's 2025 pole at the same track. Mercedes' own Toto Wolff warned pre-season that early simulations showed gaps "up to ten seconds" before development closed part of it.

Source: F1 Newsauto and Scuderiafans, both citing official qualifying times for the same circuits in 2025 and 2026.

04"Disgraceful Teletubby engineers"

The Hungarian Grand Prix produced a blue-flag mess so confusing that Mercedes boss Toto Wolff went off on race control over the radio mid-session, unprompted, about the marshalling. International media ran with it for days. Somewhere in the same weekend, Max Verstappen was independently explaining his own dive-bomb overtaking logic on a completely unrelated pass on Lewis Hamilton.

Source: RacingNews365, Hungarian GP race-week coverage.

Filed under: bureaucracy of speed

A 10th place that lasted about an hour

Pérez's Monaco result is the cleanest example this season of how a single grid-alignment technicality can erase a result that already happened, on television, in front of everyone.

Two teams, one overheating problem, two race weekends

Cadillac lost both cars to the exact same brake overheating issue at back-to-back Grands Prix (Austria, then Hungary) — not a coincidence anyone at the team seems thrilled to discuss.

A team radio outburst about children's television

Comparing your own race control to Teletubbies, on an open broadcast channel, during a live Grand Prix, is a very specific kind of professional confidence.
