A tradition older than most of its own references

The original "Treehouse of Horror" aired October 25, 1990 — inspired by 1950s EC Comics horror tales. This year's "Treehouse of Horror XXXVII" aired on October 25, 2026: the 36th anniversary, to the day.

WHAT GETS PARODIED HAS QUIETLY FLIPPED

Every segment, categorized by source material and grouped into five-year windows. The share of each category tells the real story.

In 1990–94, classic horror and literary sources made up 60% of segments. By 2015–19, modern franchise and pop-culture parody had grown to 60% — while literary classics had dropped to zero.

The genre that explains the shift

Early seasons leaned on EC Comics-style horror and literary dread: Poe, the Brothers Grimm, Frankenstein, Dracula. Modern seasons reach for whatever just hit theaters: Parasite, Jurassic Park, Spider-Verse, Pacific Rim. The show didn't get less scary — it started parodying blockbusters instead of books.

THE REPEAT OFFENDERS

Some source material gets parodied once and retired. A handful of names come back again and again, decades apart.

Stephen King has been parodied across 9 separate segments spanning 32 years — from "The Shinning" (1994) to a "Pet Sematary" riff in this year's special. No other single author comes close.

The 34-year callback nobody asked for

The very first Treehouse of Horror segment in 1990 was built on Edgar Allan Poe's "The Raven." The show didn't touch Poe again until 2024's "The Fall of the House of Monty" — a riff on "The Fall of the House of Usher," 34 years later.

Why it changed

Starting with season 34 (2022), Fox began airing two Halloween specials some years instead of one — a sign the franchise had outgrown its own three-segment box. 2024 was the extreme case: two full specials, six segments, the most Treehouse of Horror content ever produced in a single season.

"Treehouse of Horror XXXVII" parodies The Nightmare Before Christmas in its prologue, then runs two more segments: "My Husbands" (based on Holly Gramazio's novel) and "Freakish Friday" — a mashup of Freaky Friday and Stephen King's Pet Sematary.

A second special, "Treehouse of Horror Presents: The Revengening," aired a week earlier on October 18 — continuing the now-regular pattern of two Halloween outings per season.
