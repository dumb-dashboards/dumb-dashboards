The Vibe Index

Two scores for every year, 2000 to 2025. Serious: S&P 500 return, US consumer sentiment, world GDP growth, Doomsday Clock. Stupid: hot dogs eaten by the Nathan's champion, and baby girls named Felicity. Each measure is turned into a z-score, then averaged.

Sources: S&P 500 total return (Slickcharts, YCharts); University of Michigan sentiment, annual average (2024-25 averaged by us from monthly readings); World Bank GDP growth; Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists; Nathan's results (Wikipedia); SSA baby names (NameAlmanac).

The plot twist that isn't

Raw numbers say the serious and stupid indexes move in opposite directions: when the world does worse, America eats more hot dogs.

raw correlation
after removing the time trend
It's the calendar. The Doomsday Clock keeps moving closer to midnight while hot dog records keep climbing, so anything measured over time looks related. Take the trend out and the link disappears.

Prophet or mirror?

Pantone picks each color in December. Does it predict the year ahead (prophet), or reflect the year that just ended (mirror)? Here is the strongest test we ran: color lightness against the serious index.

Methodology and sources

Colors: hex approximations of each Pantone Color of the Year (Wikipedia, W3Schools); 2026 Cloud Dancer #F0EEE9. Saturation, lightness and warmth come from HSL; warmth weights the hue's distance from orange by saturation. Years with two colors use the average. Indexes: each series is standardized over 1999-2025 and averaged; the Doomsday Clock counts seconds to midnight in force during the year. Prophet: color of year Y against index of Y. Mirror: color of year Y, chosen in December of Y-1, against index of Y-1. Significance via 20,000 random permutations. Nathan's switched from 12 to 10 minutes in 2008. Felicity is measured per 1,000 girls born. Correlation is not causation, and in this case it isn't even correlation.
