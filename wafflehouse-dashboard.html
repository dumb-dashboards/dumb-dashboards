The Waffle House Index

How America measures disasters in hash browns. Storms, closures, a map of every state, and the math behind 1.5 million potato combinations.

A DISASTER SCALE MADE OF BREAKFAST

Waffle House is open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and is famous for staying open through storms. So emergency managers started checking one thing after a disaster: what is the local Waffle House serving?

GREEN
Full menu
Power is on, supplies are getting through. Damage is minimal.
YELLOW
Limited menu
Running on a generator or low on food. No power or water, but gas for the stove: bacon, eggs and coffee.
RED
Closed
Rare. Roads blocked, infrastructure damaged, recovery delayed. This is where help goes first.
"If you get there and the Waffle House is closed? That's really bad." — CRAIG FUGATE, FORMER FEMA ADMINISTRATOR
Definitions: FEMA blog (2017) and Craig Fugate, as reported by AP and Wikipedia.

HOW IT STARTED

Aug 2004
Hurricane Charley. Fugate, then Florida's emergency chief, can only find a Waffle House on a limited menu. The idea is born.
2005
Katrina destroys 7 restaurants and shuts 100. The ones that reopen fast boom, so the company builds its whole strategy around staying open.
May 2011
Joplin tornado. Both local Waffle Houses stay open, and Fugate, now running FEMA, uses the term in public.
2017
A FOIA request turns up emails calling it Fugate's personal project, not an official FEMA system.
Sources: Wikipedia (Waffle House Index), AP / PBS NewsHour.

Methodology and sources

The Waffle House Index is informal: FOIA emails describe it as Craig Fugate's personal project. Storm closure counts come from news reports quoting the company and are taken at different times, so they are not strictly comparable. State counts come from public store locators via Guess the Chain (October 2026); other trackers give slightly different totals. Yearly food volumes are the company's own. Per-minute rates, the egg-surcharge estimate and the hash brown combinatorics are our own calculations, labelled as such.
