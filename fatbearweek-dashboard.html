2.5 million votes, a 16-bear bracket, and a salmon river sitting on top of one of the biggest resource fights in America. Alaska's Katmai National Park, explained with actual numbers.

A mother-daughter dynasty

Backpack's mother, Bear 435 "Holly," won Fat Bear Week in 2019. Backpack got his nickname as a cub by riding on Holly's back instead of swimming alongside her.

Meanwhile, defending champion 32 "Chunk" was eliminated in the very first round by Bear 164 "Bucky" — the reigning title holder knocked out before the bracket even got interesting.

The vote count has more than tripled since 2021

From 793,463 total votes in 2021 to over 2.5 million in 2026 — Fat Bear Week's audience has grown faster than most actual sports leagues.

03From lean to legendary in 10 weeks

Adult males enter the summer feeding frenzy (hyperphagia) around 600–900 lbs in July. By Fat Bear Week in late September, some top 1,400 lbs. Adult females average around 700 lbs at peak — about a third less than males.

Source: National Geographic Kids, NPR, NPS.

Females need roughly 25% body fat at minimum to become pregnant during hibernation — for bears, "fat" is literally a reproductive requirement, not a cosmetic one.

Source: Mike Fitz / Explore.org volumetric body-scan research; AOL/Katmai 2025 coverage.

Otis's record: 240,000 calories in one sitting

4-time champion 480 Otis once ate 42 salmon in 5.5 hours — roughly 240,000 calories in a single feeding session.

That's more than a month's worth of food for an average adult human, eaten in an afternoon.

Source: Katmai National Park ranger Naomi Boak, via NPR (2023).

04The only human sport built on the same idea

Sumo wrestling is the one human sport where deliberately gaining weight is part of elite training — a strict high-calorie diet (chanko nabe, eaten in two large meals) aimed at maximizing mass, since wrestlers aren't separated into weight classes.

Source: NPR, Japan Travel Planning, Gurunavi.

Bear vs. sumo wrestler, head to head

A fall-season Fat Bear Week contender can outweigh even the heaviest sumo wrestler ever recorded (Ōrora Satoshi, 645 lbs) — and bears carry a higher body-fat percentage (~35%) than elite sumo wrestlers (~25–30%), who need to stay mobile enough to compete.

Source: Wikipedia (Ōrora Satoshi), Japan Travel Planning, Slurrp.

05The meme bears sit on top of a $2 billion fight

Katmai's Brooks River — Fat Bear Week's entire stage — is part of the Bristol Bay watershed, home to the largest wild sockeye salmon run on Earth.

The mine that almost changed everything

At the river's headwaters sat the proposed Pebble Mine — a gold, copper, and molybdenum deposit worth "hundreds of billions" of dollars. Opponents warned it would destroy the salmon spawning habitat that feeds both the commercial fishery and every bear in the park.

In 2023, the EPA invoked the Clean Water Act to block the mine — a rare case of environmentalists, commercial fishermen, sport fishers, and Alaska Native communities all on the same side, across party lines.

Source: EPA, Bristol Bay Native Corporation, Wild Salmon Center.

The same fish, two very different price tags

What a Bristol Bay fisherman gets paid per pound of sockeye has swung wildly — from a peak of $2.11/lb in 1988 to just $0.42/lb in 2001, driven mostly by competition from farmed salmon. The bears, notably, have never once worried about the farmed-salmon market.

Source: Wikipedia, "Dillingham, Alaska."

Methodology note

Vote totals and bracket results are as reported by Katmai National Park, Explore.org, and contemporaneous news coverage of the 2026 tournament. Bear weight and calorie figures are field estimates from NPS rangers and researchers, not laboratory measurements — bears are not, understandably, cooperative with precise scientific instruments. Economic figures for Bristol Bay are the most recently published totals from cited sources and may shift year to year with salmon returns and market prices.
