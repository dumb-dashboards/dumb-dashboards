The 2027 follow-up, announced 9 days later

Oasis Live '27 was confirmed September 14, 2026 — nine days after the premiere of "Oasis: Don't Look Back in Anger," the documentary chronicling the 2025 reunion (the tour itself had actually wrapped ten months earlier, in São Paulo on November 23, 2025). New cities include Glasgow, Munich, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Paris, and Rome in Europe, plus Boston and Las Vegas in North America — and a return to Knebworth, 31 years after the band's legendary 1996 shows there. Demand was high enough that the band added extra dates on September 24, expanding the tour to 42 shows across 9 countries.

Source: Wikipedia (Oasis Live '27 Tour, Oasis Live '25 Tour), NME, DIY Magazine, Consequence, Big Country News.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer commented publicly, calling it "not just an Oasis problem." The UK Competition and Markets Authority formally investigated.

Source: TIME, The Conversation, Rolling Stone AU.

The verdict: not "dynamic," but not transparent either

The official UK investigation found Ticketmaster did not use real-time demand-based dynamic pricing on the Oasis shows — but it did use a tiered pricing system that wasn't clearly communicated, prompting Ticketmaster to make voluntary changes to how prices are displayed.

For the North American leg, the band explicitly opted out of dynamic pricing altogether, citing the UK backlash directly in their own statement.

Source: Pollstar, NBC News.

Same tour, wildly different resale markets

Dublin and the invite-only Wembley add-on dates had the fewest resale listings and the highest starting prices. Once the tour left the UK, the picture flipped — Toronto and the New Jersey shows had tens of thousands of resale listings, with prices as low as £36.

Source: SeatPick resale market analysis.

Not even Toronto's rain delay changed it — the band played through a downpour rather than cut a single song. The only night-to-night variation was in between-song crowd dedications and a Welsh-language joke added to "Wonderwall" for the Cardiff shows.

Source: setlist.fm via Uproxx, Time Out UK, Billboard Canada.

The fixed four-song encore

Every night, without exception: "The Masterplan" → "Don't Look Back in Anger" → "Wonderwall" → "Champagne Supernova." Opening song was always "Hello."

05Where the setlist actually comes from

6 of 23 songs come from 1994's Definitely Maybe, 8 from 1995's (What's the Story) Morning Glory? — over 60% of the whole set from just their first two albums. Only 2 songs date from 2000 or later, out of a 7-album catalogue.

Source: Billboard Canada (Toronto setlist breakdown).

Two whole albums, zero representation

Nothing at all from the band's final two studio albums — Don't Believe the Truth (2005) and Dig Out Your Soul (2008) — made the Live '25 set. A small handful of B-sides and cuts from 1997's Be Here Now filled out the rest, alongside the two solitary post-2000 songs.

Source: Billboard (Oasis 2027 tour setlist predictions).

06Liam fronts it — but Noel gets his own mini-set

Live reviews confirm the show carves out a dedicated stretch — bookended by "Roll With It" and "Stand By Me" — where Noel Gallagher takes lead vocals on his own songs, a structural break from Liam's frontman role for the rest of the night.

Noel also sings lead on "Don't Look Back in Anger" itself, in the encore — a song he wrote and has sung live since 1996.

Source: Yahoo Entertainment (Live '25 tour review, MetLife Stadium).

The one time Liam ever sang it himself

"Don't Look Back in Anger" has been a Noel-sung song for its entire 29-year history. Liam performed it solo exactly once — an a cappella version at Glastonbury 2017. On Live '25, Noel sings it, as always.

Source: Wikipedia, "Don't Look Back in Anger."

The reunion's actual opening statement

"Acquiesce" — one of only a couple of Oasis songs written for shared Liam/Noel vocals, the two brothers trading lines back and forth — was played as the 2nd song, every single night, right after "Hello." For a tour built entirely around the brothers reuniting, it's hard to imagine a more literal choice.

"Let There Be Love" is the other well-known Liam/Noel duet — but it's a Don't Believe the Truth (2005) track, and that whole album got zero representation on Live '25, so it wasn't part of this setlist.

Source: setlist.fm via Uproxx (confirmed Cardiff opening-night and full-tour setlist).

07The real surprise: a band famous for chaos, playing it perfectly safe

Oasis built their reputation on unpredictability — onstage fights, cancelled shows, Liam walking off mid-tour. A reunion this fragile, many assumed, would see the setlist shift, songs get dropped in anger, something go wrong. Instead: the exact same 23 songs, in the exact same order, at all 41 shows, on 5 continents, without a single change.

Multiple reviewers flagged this consistency itself as the story — not because it's unusual for a tour in general, but because it's unusual for this band specifically.

Source: setlist.fm via Uproxx, Time Out UK.

For Live '27: informed guesses, not confirmed setlists

Billboard's own 2027 predictions suggest a Liam-led ballad like "Stop Crying Your Heart Out" could replace a Noel-led track — and note the band was overheard soundchecking extra songs during Live '25 that never made the actual show.

None of this is confirmed. Live '27 has not begun; treat every prediction in this card as speculation until the tour actually starts.

Source: Billboard, "Oasis 2027 Tour Setlist Predictions."
