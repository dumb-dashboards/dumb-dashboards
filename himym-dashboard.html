The finale is, statistically, the worst thing the show ever did

"Last Forever," the series finale, aired as two parts back to back. Part 1 scored 6.5. Part 2 scored 6.0 — the single lowest-rated episode of the entire series, out of 208. The show's most-watched episode ever (13.1M viewers) is also, by audience rating, its least-loved.

03The Slap Bet, rated episode by episode

S2E9 "Slap Bet" · S3E9 "Slapsgiving" · S5E9 "Slapsgiving 2" · S7E9 "Disaster Averted" · S9E14 "Slapsgiving 3" · S9E22 "The End of the Aisle". The show's single highest-rated episode ever (8.6) is the one that started this joke. Its own sequels decline almost in a straight line, bottoming out at 6.5 for "Slapsgiving 3" — a running gag that quietly plots the whole series' arc in miniature.

A coincidence the writers definitely planned

The first four slaps land on "Slap Bet" (S2E9), "Slapsgiving" (S3E9), "Slapsgiving 2" (S5E9), and "Disaster Averted" (S7E9) — the ninth episode of the season, four different seasons in a row. That's not a coincidence a viewer would catch live; it's a writers'-room easter egg for anyone who checks the numbers.

Barney's number, tracked in real time by the show itself

S4E22 "Right Place, Right Time" (200) → S6E3 "Unfinished" (236) → S6E22 "The Perfect Cocktail" (282+). At a steady pace, that's roughly one new "conquest" every 8–9 days for four straight years.

The Bro Code

At least 83 numbered rules — the Platinum Rule is literally Article 83. The number 83 itself becomes a running joke, reused as a fake statistic ("83% success rate") more than half a dozen times across the series.

The Playbook

Barney's published seduction manual documents 75+ named "plays" — each with a listed success rate, target type, and required prep time, played entirely straight as a parody business manual.
